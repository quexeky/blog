This is going to be a bit of a brain dump but here goes:
Today I've talked to two people, one giving out grants who, from I understand, started gunning for startups when he was 16, and the other an already very accomplished 3rd year at uni, working on building a business and making products, while also making a very cool underwater robot. Both asked me similar questions about why I do engineering in the way that I do: why do I make and choose the pathway that I have chosen, and why do I not use LLMs to speed up my progress (among many other questions, but these are the relevant ones here). I think my answer is thus:

I do it for the love of the game.

I engineer, I design, I build, simply because it is what I do. I fell in love with robotics about 3-4 years ago, but have always loved approaching a problem technically. Always loved learning and tearing apart and rebuilding anything that I come across, simply for the sake of understanding the process behind its original design. It's that obsession with learning that makes me never regret a second spent doing the painful thing after it's done, that makes me regret taking shortcuts (like using an LLM to design a system). 

If someone said to me "You're set for life, do what you want," I would engineer (is that the correct way to phrase that? "Engineer"? I'm not sure. Deal with it). Someone hasn't said that to me yet, and still, I engineer. To some degree, it's a form of self expression, like an art form. Anything that I make reflects who I am as a person because they are all built exactly the way that I wanted them too. If I want to build something scrappy and fast, then I'll build it scrappy and fast and cut the corners that I want to. If I want to build something perfect, then I'll agonise over every single detail until every API is as intuitive as I want it to be, every joint works as smoothly as I think it should, and every very over-engineered frame that cost way too much because it seemed cool to built it that way, will give me joy in calculating how many kilos I could put onto it (for my current project, the gantry, that's about 120kg at the weakest point, and 4000 at the strongest from memory).

Sure, an LLM is useful. I'll use them to automate the boring tasks, or the ones that I need to be done quickly. Sometimes I'll use it to review work (albeit, I've since learnt to not do it for blog posts because they come out much less interesting afterwards). But to me, the point of the game isn't necessarily to finish making something. It's that feeling of learning, of doing something new, that I always search for. Why do I write these posts? Because I want to learn to put my feelings out with *relative* conciseness (if you've ever listened to me talk in person, you'd know what I mean). Why do I want to force myself through long and possibly expensive projects? Because it gives me a way to focus on learning something without being distracted by whatever else there may be around me. 

That being said about finishing things, there's also a joy in doing that too - in seeing so much suffering come through in a final creation that is marvelous to me, even if it's something as simple as my first 3D design, because *I* did that. 

I'm probably repeating myself a lot here ;-;

I don't know why it is that I feel this way, but I know that I do, and I don't think I'd ever change that, because it gives me reason to do so much more than I ever would without it.



/rant over
